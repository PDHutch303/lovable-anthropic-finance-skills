# Finance Skill Quality Standard

Apply this standard to every material, review-facing, tax-sensitive, audit-sensitive, investment, family-office, or CFO workflow in this repository.

## 1. Pin the scope before analysis

Establish and preserve:
- legal entity or reporting group;
- period and comparison period;
- accounting basis (cash, accrual, tax, management, or other);
- closed-through / data-as-of date;
- currency;
- materiality or decision threshold when relevant;
- source systems and source files.

Do not silently mix entities, periods, bases, currencies, or data vintages.

## 2. Evidence and provenance

Prefer evidence in this order when sources conflict:
1. authoritative external evidence such as bank/custodian statements, signed agreements, tax forms, lender statements, official notices, and third-party confirmations;
2. reconciled subledgers and schedules;
3. general ledger / trial balance / accounting-system reports;
4. management schedules and exports;
5. emails, notes, and user recollection;
6. estimates or assumptions.

Preserve source references and distinguish **fact**, **calculation**, **estimate**, **assumption**, and **professional judgment**. Never upgrade an estimate into a fact because it makes a schedule tie.

## 3. Pull once, calculate once

When several deliverables use the same figures, use one pinned source set and reuse the same derived calculations. Do not independently re-pull or retype the same period in multiple workflows unless a fresh pull is intentionally requested and time-stamped.

## 4. Tie-outs before narrative

Run the cheapest decisive integrity tests before writing conclusions. Examples:
- debits = credits;
- bank adjusted balance = adjusted book balance;
- AR/AP aging = control account;
- subledger or roll-forward = GL;
- beginning + activity = ending;
- intercompany due-to = counterpart due-from;
- consolidated totals = entities + eliminations;
- inventory roll-forward = GL and supporting quantity/value records;
- capital calls/distributions/NAV = investment roll-forward;
- return/workpaper totals = books and tax forms;
- waterfall tiers = total distributable cash;
- bridge/variance drivers = total movement.

A failed tie-out is a finding or blocker. Never create a balancing plug merely to clear it.

## 5. Missing data degrades the answer; it does not authorize invention

When evidence is missing:
- say what is missing;
- state what conclusion it blocks or weakens;
- continue with the portions the evidence can support;
- label any provisional assumption;
- identify the document or system that would resolve it.

Do not refuse useful work simply because the package is incomplete, but do not imply completeness that the evidence cannot support.

## 6. Materiality and consequentiality

Use explicit materiality when evaluating financial-statement errors, close exceptions, audit items, covenant issues, or variance follow-up. Do not hide individually small items when their aggregate, control implication, fraud risk, or qualitative significance is material.

Consequential actions require stronger evidence and review even when the dollar amount is small, including wire changes, tax elections, entity ownership, legal rights, covenant compliance, quality/audit opinions, and release of funds.

## 7. Exception discipline

Every unresolved item should have, where applicable:
- issue;
- amount/exposure;
- source/evidence;
- owner;
- status;
- next action;
- due date;
- financial/reporting impact;
- whether it blocks completion.

Do not delete exceptions because they became immaterial or were accepted. Preserve resolution history.

## 8. Human approval boundary

Safe automation generally includes extraction, normalization, matching suggestions, calculations, draft schedules, exception identification, draft entries, draft reports, and draft communications.

Require explicit authorization or qualified-professional review before consequential external actions such as:
- posting or deleting material accounting entries when approval is required;
- filing tax returns or elections;
- initiating payments or changing banking instructions;
- issuing audit/assurance opinions;
- making legal conclusions;
- approving investment trades or individualized securities recommendations;
- changing ownership or trust/estate records;
- finalizing valuations represented as appraisals/fairness opinions.

## 9. Current-authority rule

Tax law, accounting standards, audit standards, filing requirements, rates, thresholds, deadlines, and regulations are time-sensitive. For material conclusions:
- identify jurisdiction and tax/reporting period;
- verify current authoritative guidance;
- record the source and access/effective date;
- distinguish authoritative material from commentary;
- flag qualified-professional review points.

Software acceptance is not authority.

## 10. Review-ready deliverable

A strong output should contain, as applicable:
1. scope and source set;
2. what the records show;
3. tie-out/reconciliation status;
4. exceptions and missing evidence;
5. calculations and assumptions;
6. conclusion or decision options;
7. journal entries/schedules/models if relevant;
8. financial, cash, tax, covenant, ownership, or investment impact;
9. review/approval required;
10. next actions with owner and deadline.

## 11. Specialty workflows inherit the core controls

Industry-specific or family-office skills add domain logic; they do not replace accounting integrity. A manufacturing, medical, construction, trust, PE, or service-company analysis still follows source, scope, tie-out, exception, approval, and authority rules above.

## 12. Quality gate

Before calling work complete, ask:
- Is the entity/period/basis/data-as-of clear?
- Does every material figure trace to evidence or a labeled calculation?
- Do the relevant schedules tie?
- Are unresolved differences visible?
- Are assumptions and judgments distinguished from facts?
- Have stale/time-sensitive rules been verified?
- Are approval boundaries respected?
- Could another qualified reviewer reproduce the conclusion from the cited evidence?

If any answer is no, disclose the limitation or keep the work open.
