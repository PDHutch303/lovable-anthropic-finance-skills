# Manufacturing Cost Accounting

Follow `QUALITY-STANDARD.md`.

## Map the cost flow
Purchasing/receipts → raw material → issues to production → WIP → completed units/finished goods → shipment → COGS. Identify subledgers/systems for quantities, standard/actual cost, labor and GL postings.

## Period-end controls
1. Reconcile RM, WIP and FG quantity/value subledgers to GL control accounts.
2. Roll forward beginning inventory + receipts/production - issues/completions/shipments ± adjustments = ending inventory by stage.
3. Test cutoff around receipts, completions and shipments.
4. Investigate negative inventory, stale/open work orders, zero/negative-cost items and manual inventory adjustments.
5. Review standard-cost changes/effectivity and whether revaluation was appropriate.
6. Analyze purchase-price, material usage, labor rate/efficiency and overhead spending/volume/absorption variances where the system supports them.
7. Review scrap/rework and obsolete/slow-moving reserve evidence.
8. Reconcile manufacturing variances and inventory movement to COGS/gross-margin movement before writing explanations.

## Overhead
Document the allocation base, budgeted/normal capacity and included cost pools. Unsupported overhead rates or persistent under/over-absorption should be surfaced; do not bury them in inventory to stabilize margin.

## Output
Provide RM/WIP/FG/COGS bridge, GL tie-out, open work-order/WIP aging, major variances, reserve analysis, gross-margin impact, proposed entries and operations questions.

## Guardrails
- Do not infer physical existence from ledger quantity alone.
- Do not force standard and actual cost to agree; explain variances.
- Do not allocate unexplained variance to WIP/COGS merely to close.
- For GAAP/tax inventory conclusions, verify applicable current accounting/tax guidance and policy.
