# Quality of Earnings

Follow `QUALITY-STANDARD.md`.

QoE is an evidence-backed bridge from reported earnings to normalized earnings, not a collection of optimistic add-backs.

## Preflight
Pin entity/perimeter, periods, accounting basis, reported EBITDA/net income definition, materiality, source TB/GL, financial statements and diligence data. Tie reported EBITDA to the underlying books before adjustments.

## Analyze earnings quality
Review:
- revenue cutoff, returns/credits, concentration and unusual end-period activity;
- gross margin/product/customer/location mix where data supports it;
- owner/related-party and discretionary items;
- genuinely nonrecurring income/expense;
- missing/understated recurring costs;
- run-rate changes with effective date and evidence;
- payroll/headcount normalization;
- leases/rent and related-party market adjustments only with support;
- accounting policy/classification differences;
- working-capital seasonality and potential peg issues;
- cash-to-earnings conversion and unusual balance-sheet movements.

## Adjustment standard
For each adjustment show description, period, amount, sign, evidence, rationale, recurring/nonrecurring/run-rate classification, buyer/seller sensitivity, and whether it is an accounting correction versus a normalization. Avoid double counting one fact in multiple adjustments.

Reported EBITDA +/− discrete supported adjustments = adjusted EBITDA. The bridge must close exactly.

## Output
Provide reported-to-adjusted bridge, adjustment register, revenue/gross-margin findings, working-capital observations, concentration/cash conversion, unresolved diligence requests and sensitivity range.

## Guardrails
- An add-back is not valid merely because management labels it one-time.
- Do not annualize temporary gains without evidence of persistence.
- Separate accounting errors/cutoff corrections from normalization.
- QoE is not an audit or assurance opinion and should identify limitations of source data.
